The Complete Google Business Profile Optimization Checklist for Knoxville Businesses

Your Google Business Profile is the single most important thing determining whether your business shows up on Google Maps and in local search results. Not your website. Not your ads. Your GBP.

I audit local businesses in Knoxville every week — plumbers, roofers, HVAC contractors, tree services — and the Google Business Profile is where I start every single time. It is also where I find the most fixable problems.

Most business owners set up their profile when they first opened, filled in a name and phone number, and never touched it again. Then they wonder why their competitors are showing up instead of them.

Google gives you dozens of tools to tell it exactly what your business does, where you operate, and why you deserve to show up. Most businesses use about three of them.

Here is the full checklist, in order of impact, based on what I actually find and fix in real audits.

• • •

Set One Primary Category — and Only One

Your Google Business Profile category is the single strongest signal Google uses to decide what searches you show up for. And the number one mistake I see is businesses loading up five, six, seven categories thinking it helps.

It does not. It dilutes your signal. If you are a plumber, your primary category should be "Plumber." That is it. Not "Plumber, Water Heater Installation, Drain Cleaning Service, General Contractor." Google sees all that and does not know what you actually are.

I recommend one primary category. At most three. When I audited Wolf's Roofing in Oak Ridge, they had too many categories listed — roofing, plumbing, flooring contractor, and more. Stripping it down to one is the first thing I would fix. On the other hand, Monster Tree Service of Knoxville had exactly one category and it was exactly right.

Fill Out Your Services — All of Them

So if you only have one category, how do you tell Google about everything else you do? Services.

Google lets you add up to 99 services to your profile. Most businesses I audit have zero. This is the single biggest missed opportunity in local SEO because it is completely free and takes about an hour.

I recommend adding at least 30 services with written descriptions for your top 20. If you are a plumber, that means kitchen plumbing, bathroom plumbing, water heater installation, tankless water heater repair, sewer line inspection, drain cleaning, garbage disposal repair, faucet replacement — every specific thing you do gets its own entry.

This is how you capture search queries like "tankless water heater repair Knoxville" without diluting your primary category. Google reads these service entries and matches them against what people are searching for. If the service is not listed, Google does not know you do it.

Add Your Booking Link

This sounds basic but a surprising number of businesses do not have it. The booking link is the button on your Google profile that lets someone contact you with one click. Without it, a potential customer has to find your phone number, copy it, switch apps, and call. Every step you add loses people.

Point the booking link to your "Contact Us" page or your scheduling tool. When I audited Archer Air Conditioning in Powell, they were already ranking in the top three but had no booking link set up. That single missing button was the easiest fix in the entire audit. On the flip side, Friendly Neighborhood Plumber had theirs working perfectly — and it showed in how easy it was to go from finding them to contacting them.

Answer Every Question Google Asks You

When you log into your Google Business Profile, Google asks you dozens of questions. Do you offer free estimates? Are you veteran-owned? Do you have wheelchair accessibility? What payment methods do you accept? Do you serve customers at their location?

Most business owners skip these because they feel tedious. And they are tedious. But every answer you give is another data point Google uses to understand your business and match you with search queries. With AI-generated summaries now pulling directly from your GBP, those answers are literally being served to potential customers before they even click on your profile.

The businesses that give Google the most to work with get the most visibility. Fill out everything. Yes, all of it.

Get Your Review Velocity Right

Reviews are not just social proof for customers. They are a direct ranking signal for Google. And what matters is not how many you have — it is how consistently new ones are coming in.

Google weighs one review from last week higher than ten reviews from a year ago. I have seen a business with 56 reviews outrank a competitor with over 800, specifically because the smaller business had fresh reviews coming in every few days while the larger one had gone stale.

What I aim for with my clients is two to three new reviews per week. Not a push where you get 30 in one month and then nothing for six months. A steady rhythm. A simple system — a text or email with your direct Google review link after every completed job — is enough to keep it running without thinking about it.

Upload Photos Regularly and Geotag Them

Photos increase your engagement rate — the percentage of people who see your profile and actually interact with it. They also increase your activity rate, which Google treats as a trust signal. A profile with fresh photos looks alive. A profile with the same three photos from 2022 looks abandoned.

I recommend uploading different types of photos: completed jobs, before-and-after shots, your crew at work, your trucks, your storefront or office. And geotag every one with GPS coordinates from the job site. While geotagging is debated in the SEO industry, I always recommend it. It helps with proximity-based searches and there is no downside to doing it.

When I audited Wolf's Roofing in Oak Ridge, they had zero photos on their profile. That is an immediate fix — even uploading ten photos in one afternoon would improve their profile.

Write a Real Business Description

Google gives you 750 characters for your business description. Use all of them. Include your primary service, your city, the areas you serve, and what makes you different. Do not keyword-stuff it — write it for a real person who is reading it to decide whether to call you.

This is also where you can mention things like how long you have been in business, whether you are locally owned, what your service area covers, and what your specialty is. Google reads this. Customers read this. Make it count.

Add FAQ Posts to Your Profile

Google lets you add questions and answers directly to your GBP. Most businesses have zero. I recommend adding at least five to ten that match the questions your actual customers ask you — "Do you offer free estimates?" "What areas do you serve?" "Do you handle emergency calls?" "Are you licensed and insured?"

These FAQ entries give Google even more content to work with and they show up directly on your profile where customers can see them. With Google's AI now generating summaries from your profile data, these answers are increasingly likely to be surfaced automatically.

• • •

The Profile Completeness Trap

One thing I want to address because I see it confuse business owners: Google shows you a "profile completeness" percentage with a green circle. Business owners see that circle is not at 100% and panic.

That green circle is largely a vanity metric. Google pushes it because several of the actions that "complete" your profile involve spending money — running Google Ads, enabling Google's messaging features, and so on. A complete profile by Google's definition is not the same as an optimized profile by SEO standards.

Focus on the checklist above, not the green circle. The things that actually move your ranking are categories, services, reviews, photos, booking links, attributes, and NAP consistency — not whether you have turned on Google's chat feature.

What Happens When You Get This Right

The businesses ranking in the top three on Google Maps in Knoxville right now are not doing anything magical. They have just done more of these things, more correctly, more recently than the businesses below them.

Every item on this checklist is a concrete action you can take this week. None of them require a new website. None of them cost money. None of them take more than an afternoon. And each one is a signal that tells Google: this business is real, it is active, and it deserves to show up when someone searches for what it does.
