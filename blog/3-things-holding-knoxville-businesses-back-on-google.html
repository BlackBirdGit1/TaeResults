3 Things Holding Most Knoxville Businesses Back on Google

July 2026 · Knoxville, TN

I audit local businesses in Knoxville every week and I keep finding the same three problems over and over. I recorded a short video walking through all three — watch it below, then keep reading for the full breakdown.

• • •

1. Your Google Business Profile is half-empty.

Google gives you up to 99 service slots on your profile. Most businesses I audit have zero filled in. Google asks you dozens of questions — payment methods, accessibility, service highlights. Most are blank. There is a booking link feature that lets customers contact you with one click. Most do not have it set up.

Every empty field is a missed signal. Google is deciding who deserves the top three spots, and it uses everything you give it. When your profile is half-complete, you are telling Google you are half a business.

I found this exact problem in a Powell HVAC company that was already ranking in the top three. They had no booking link, no schema on their website, and their GBP services were not filled out. They were winning despite those gaps — but any competitor who fills theirs in could take that spot.

The fix: go into your Google Business Profile and fill in every single field. Add at least 30 services with descriptions. Set up the booking link. Answer every question Google asks. This alone can move your ranking.

2. Your reviews have gone stale.

You might have 200 reviews. That sounds great. But Google does not just count reviews — it weighs how recent they are. One review from last week matters more to Google's algorithm than ten reviews from a year ago.

I have seen businesses with 56 reviews outrank competitors with 800-plus — specifically because the smaller business had newer, more consistent reviews coming in. What I look for in every audit is velocity. Are reviews coming in every week? Or did they all come in during one push six months ago and then stop?

When I audited Friendly Neighborhood Plumber here in Knoxville, their review velocity was one of the strongest things about their profile. They had a system. That is the difference.

The fix: set up a system. A direct Google review link sent to every customer after every completed job. Aim for two to three per week. Not a one-time push — a rhythm.

3. Your website is not telling Google what you do.

Your website might look great. Professional design, nice photos, clean layout. But Google does not see what you see. Google reads code.

What Google is looking for is very specific. Your title tag — the text that shows up in the browser tab — needs to include your main service and your city. Your H1 heading needs to match. Your address and phone number need to be hard-coded in the footer of every page, not buried in an image or hidden behind a "Contact Us" link.

When I audited Friendly Neighborhood Plumber, their title tag and H1 were both perfect — "Plumbing Service in Knoxville, Tennessee." That is exactly what Google wants to see. On the other hand, one Oak Ridge roofing company I reviewed did not have a website at all. Businesses from Knoxville — twenty minutes away — were ranking in Oak Ridge instead of them.

The fix: check your title tag (the text in your browser tab). Does it say "[Your Service] in [Your City]"? Is your H1 heading the same? Is your address in the footer as plain text? Is your mobile speed score above 70?
