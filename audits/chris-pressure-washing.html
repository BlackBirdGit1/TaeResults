Chris's Pressure Washing Local SEO Audit

Moorestown, TN · Pressure Washing

Chris's Pressure Washing serves the Moorestown and Johnson City area in Tennessee and has a professionally built website with a strong content foundation. But right now, when someone Googles their business name, other businesses and pages are competing for that front page real estate. That is not a brand problem — it is a domain authority problem. And domain authority is built by doing the exact things this audit covers: fixing the technical gaps, building citations, and giving Google more reasons to trust this business. The good news is the foundation is already strong — the gaps are specific and fixable.

We audited Chris's Pressure Washing, a pressure washing business serving Moorestown, Johnson City, and surrounding areas in East Tennessee. How Chris's Pressure Washing Appears on Google Right Now

We started by Googling their business name to check for brand competition. Unlike most businesses we audit, Chris's Pressure Washing is competing for their own name on the front page. Other businesses and pages with similar names are diluting their search position. This is happening because the domain authority for Chris's Pressure Washing is not high enough yet for Google to give them clear ownership of that front page.

This is fixable. Domain authority increases when you add high-quality content, build citations on trusted directories, earn backlinks from other reputable websites, and maintain consistent activity on your Google Business Profile. Every recommendation in this audit contributes to solving this problem — it is not one fix, it is the accumulation of all of them.

Chris's Pressure Washing Google Business Profile Review

Reviews tell a mixed story here. Chris's Pressure Washing clearly had a review system working at some point — the pattern of consistent reviews is visible, and the business was responsive to them, which is exactly what you want to see. But the most recent review is from over a year ago. Whatever system was generating those reviews stopped working or stopped being used.

This is one of the most impactful things to fix immediately. Google weighs one review from yesterday higher than ten reviews from a year ago. The review velocity that was working before needs to be restarted. A direct Google review link sent to every customer after every completed job, consistently, aiming for two to three new reviews per week. The system clearly worked before — the fix is just turning it back on.

Photos are in good shape. Chris's Pressure Washing has a strong collection of before-and-after shots, which is exactly the type of content that increases engagement from potential customers browsing the profile. Diverse photo types and consistent posting send activity signals to Google. Geotagging each photo with GPS coordinates from the job site helps with proximity-based searches as well.

For GBP categories, we recommend one primary category, at most three. The key principle is making it clear to Google what the business does and where it does it — that theme runs through everything in local SEO. Google also provides up to 99 service slots. We recommend filling in at least 30 with descriptions for the top 20. Services are how you capture the broad spectrum of search queries — someone searching "deck cleaning Johnson City" will only find Chris's Pressure Washing through the services section, not through the category alone.

The booking link should be set up if it is not already. It lowers friction for potential customers and gives you another way to track whether your GBP is actually converting views into contacts. Beyond categories, services, and booking, every field and attribute Google asks about should be completed. More data means more confidence from Google's algorithm.

Chris's Pressure Washing Website SEO Review

The website was clearly done professionally — clean design, good structure, service pages built out, service areas listed in the footer. The foundation is solid. But there are specific on-page SEO gaps holding it back.

The title tag reads "Professional Exterior Cleaning." That is a fine description, but it is missing the most important signal: the location. Google needs to see what you do AND where you do it. The title tag should include the service type plus the primary city — something like "Pressure Washing Johnson City TN — Chris's Pressure Washing." The same applies to the H1 tag. Johnson City appears on the page, but it is not coded as the H1 heading. Making the H1 explicitly state the primary keyword plus city tells Google exactly what the page is about.

There is a map image on the website showing the service area, which was a good idea. But a static image does not give Google any usable data. Replacing that image with an actual embedded Google Map would help Google link the website to the business's physical service area, strengthening the proximity signal for local searches. This is the same recommendation we made in the Archer Air Conditioning audit where a static map illustration was missing the proximity benefit a live embed provides.

Chris's Pressure Washing Technical SEO

Indexed pages are a genuine strength. Chris's Pressure Washing has a significant number of pages indexed by Google — well above the 50 we typically recommend. They have dedicated pages for specific services like deck and fence cleaning, concrete cleaning, and others, plus area-specific pages for the towns they serve. Each indexed page is a content opportunity. If someone searches "deck cleaning Johnson City Tennessee," Chris's Pressure Washing can compete for that query because they have a page targeting it. This is one of the strongest parts of the audit.

Page speed is the biggest technical problem. Mobile PageSpeed scored 38, which is well below the 70 minimum we look for. Desktop is also slow. Page speed is one of the few ranking factors Google has explicitly confirmed affects search position, and a score of 38 means there are significant backend code issues — likely oversized images, render-blocking scripts, unused CSS, or heavy third-party resources — dragging the load time down. The website looks fine visually, but the underlying code needs optimization. For comparison, Mighty Dog Roofing of Knoxville scored 78 on mobile — that gap alone is a competitive disadvantage.

Schema markup is missing. Chris's Pressure Washing does not have a LocalBusiness schema on their website. Schema is business information — name, address, phone number, services, hours, service areas — packaged in a structured format that Google can read instantly without guessing. Adding schema removes ambiguity and gives Google a cleaner, more confident read of the business data. This is a straightforward implementation that should be done as soon as possible.

Chris's Pressure Washing Citations & Local Listings

Citations are directly tied to the brand competition problem we identified at the top of this audit. When other businesses are competing for your name on Google's front page, citations are how you build enough authority for Google to give you clear ownership of that space.

Chris's Pressure Washing has some basic citations — Yelp, BBB, and Facebook appear to be in place. But the basics are the floor, not the ceiling. Google builds confidence in your business by seeing your information confirmed consistently across multiple trusted platforms. The more directories that list your business with identical name, address, and phone number, the stronger that trust signal becomes.

We build citations across 50 default high-quality directories for our clients, plus industry-specific and location-specific listings. For Chris's Pressure Washing specifically, building out citations would serve double duty — it strengthens ranking signals AND helps push competing pages off the brand name search. This is one of the highest-impact fixes available, similar to what we recommended in the Monster Tree Service of Knoxville audit where citation building was the next layer of authority needed to close the gap.

What Chris's Pressure Washing Is Doing Well

Chris's Pressure Washing has a professionally built website with strong content depth — well above 50 indexed pages covering specific services and areas. Their GBP has a history of active review generation and responsive engagement. Their before-and-after photos are strong. The footer has service areas listed. The website structure with dedicated service and area pages is exactly the playbook we follow with our own clients. This is a business with a real foundation that needs targeted technical fixes, not a complete overhaul.

Top Recommendations for Chris's Pressure Washing

1. Fix mobile page speed immediately. A score of 38 is critically low. Dig into the backend code — optimize images, defer render-blocking scripts, remove unused CSS, evaluate third-party resources. Getting this to 70+ would have the single biggest impact on rankings of anything on this list.

2. Build citations aggressively. This solves two problems at once: it strengthens ranking authority AND pushes competing pages off your brand name search. Start with the major directories and expand into niche-specific and local community listings. Aim for 50+ with identical NAP information everywhere.

3. Restart the review system. Whatever was generating consistent reviews before needs to be turned back on. A direct Google review link sent after every completed job, targeting two to three new reviews per week. The system worked — it just needs to run again.

4. Add location to the title tag and H1. Change the title tag from "Professional Exterior Cleaning" to include the primary city and business name. Make the H1 explicitly state the service plus location. This tells Google what the business does and where.

5. Add schema markup. Implement a LocalBusiness schema with name, address, phone, services, hours, and service areas. This is missing entirely and is a straightforward addition.

Bonus: Replace the static map image with an embedded Google Map, fill out at least 30 GBP services with descriptions, set up the booking link, and complete every remaining GBP attribute.
