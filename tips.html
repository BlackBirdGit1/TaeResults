3 Things Holding Most Knoxville Businesses Back on Google

July 2026 · Knoxville, TN

I audit local businesses in Knoxville every week. I record the whole thing on video — the business owner's Google profile, their website, their citations, their page speed, everything. And I keep finding the same three problems over and over.

These are not obscure technical issues. They are the three things that, if you fixed them and nothing else, would move your ranking more than anything else you could do.

• • •

1. Your Google Business Profile is half-empty.

Google gives you up to 99 service slots on your profile. Most businesses I audit have zero filled in. Google asks you dozens of questions — payment methods, accessibility, service highlights. Most are blank. There is a booking link feature that lets customers contact you with one click. Most do not have it set up.

Every empty field is a missed signal. Google is deciding who deserves the top 3 spots, and it uses everything you give it. When your profile is half-complete, you are telling Google you are half a business.

The fix: go into your Google Business Profile right now and fill in every single field. Add at least 30 services with descriptions. Set up the booking link. Answer every question Google asks. This alone can move your ranking.

But here is the thing — you do not know what your competitors have filled in. You do not know what specific fields they have completed that you have not. You do not know which gaps are actually costing you position. That is what the audit reveals.

2. Your reviews have gone stale.

You might have 200 reviews. That sounds great. But Google does not just count reviews — it weighs how recent they are. One review from last week matters more than ten from a year ago. I have seen a business with 56 reviews outrank a competitor with 800+ because the smaller business had consistent new reviews coming in.

The fix: set up a system. A direct Google review link sent to every customer after every job. Aim for two to three per week. Not a one-time push — a rhythm.

But again — you do not know your current review velocity compared to the businesses above you. You do not know whether you need two per week or five per week to catch up. The audit shows you exactly where you stand relative to your specific competitors.

3. Your website is not telling Google what you do.

Your website might look great. But Google reads code, not design. If your title tag does not include your main service and your city, Google is guessing. If your H1 heading does not match, Google is guessing. If your address and phone number are in an image instead of text, Google cannot read them at all.

The fix: check your title tag (the text in your browser tab). Does it say "[Your Service] in [Your City]"? Check your H1 heading. Is your address in the footer as text? Is your site speed above 70 on mobile?

But here is where self-diagnosis breaks down. You can check these things, but you do not know if your schema markup is right, if your NAP is consistent across 50 directories, if your citations have old addresses, or if your mobile speed score is actually below threshold. The audit catches all of it.
